# Finite-strip experiments and material identification

The material commands expose the existing `ftx_drape::strip` experiment and its shared
`strip::fit` inverse solver. A grip displacement sets the actual boundary of a finite
specimen. The production membrane elements and static Newton solver determine its
nonuniform interior deformation, total grip reactions and transverse contraction.
Grip extension is never substituted for homogeneous material strain.

## Run an existing material in the physical experiment

Both command forms accept the same `ftx-strip-experiment/1` request:

```sh
ftx materials strip-experiment request.json --json
ftx-materials strip-experiment request.json
```

The request contains `material` (an ordinary source-bearing catalog or embedded selection),
`fixture`, `extensions` and optional `controls`. The fixture declares `width_m`,
`gauge_length_m`, a unit `warp_axis` in `[across-width, along-loading]` coordinates, and
even `cells: [across, along]`. Warp loading is `[0,1]`; weft loading is `[1,0]`.
The free gauge excludes material held inside the jaws. `extensions` are engineering
changes in grip separation, between zero and two. Each is an independently initialized
quasistatic elastic equilibrium; their order does not establish a loading-history law.

The report freezes the input selection, records its request hash, and returns every
accepted state: force at both grips, free-force and force-balance residuals, midpoint
chord width, membrane energy, actual element Green-strain ranges, geometry and Newton
iterations. Source estimate admission and characterized strain domains remain enforced.
Only membrane properties are consumed. Missing density, bending, contact and transport
properties do not prevent this zero-gravity static experiment or become inferred values.

The model assumes full-width rigid no-slip grips, free interior in-plane displacement,
fixed out-of-plane displacement and no gravity. It does not characterize jaw compliance,
wrinkling, bending, irreversible recovery, rate dependence or hysteresis.

## Fit measured forces and independently qualified widths

```sh
ftx materials calibrate-strip acquisition.json --allow-estimates --json
ftx-materials calibrate-strip acquisition.json --allow-estimates
```

Use `ftx-strip-calibration/1`. The complete executable software example is
[`synthetic-clamped-strip.json`](../tests/fixtures/materials/synthetic-clamped-strip.json).
Every observation in that fixture is explicitly synthetic, generated by the production
forward experiment; its acceptance establishes software behavior, not agreement with
a measured textile. Its small mesh and numerical policy are test settings.

The input provides the original material and sources, method, conditions, analysis date,
complete experiments, search bounds, solver controls and acceptance policy. Every
experiment belongs wholly to FIT or CHECK. Each observation has an identity, grip
extension, total `force_n` and a positive `scale_n`. Optional `mid_width` provides
`value_m` and `scale_m` for the chord between the two side material points at half the
reference gauge. The scales are explicit residual weights, not inferred uncertainties.
Source acquisition aliases cannot be relabeled as independent held-out experiments.

By default, the four parameter bounds are ordered as warp stiffness in N/m, weft stiffness in N/m,
major Poisson ratio, and shear stiffness in N/m. An optional `response_family` instead identifies
three dimensionless fixed-shape scales and normal coupling in N/m; see
[declared nonlinear shape identification](clamped-strip-identification.md#identify-amplitudes-of-declared-nonlinear-shapes)
for its schema, separate shape permission and reproducible example. The optional `nonlinear`
parameterization instead identifies independent signed tangent intervals and coupling at declared
fixed Green-strain knots. It accepts 7–12 parameters, uses its own bounds, and is mutually exclusive
with `response_family`; see
[independent interval identification](clamped-strip-identification.md#identify-independent-signed-tangent-intervals).
All three modes use the same inverse solver and production membrane authority. The entire search box must be physically
admissible. Three deterministic bounded searches use only FIT observations. Publication
requires stationary starts, interior parameters, independent local sensitivity, no
discovered separated equivalent minimum, acceptable FIT and held-out CHECK errors,
doubled-mesh agreement and successful replay inside the final FIT-only strain domain.
Existing source strain limits continue to constrain replacement fits and cannot be widened
by refitting or by CHECK observations. Mesh refinement and local identifiability remain
numerical checks, not global uniqueness or statistical confidence.

`policy.max_forward_solves` charges every forward attempt, including failed trials,
sensitivity probes, refinement and final domain replay. The request also bounds states,
mesh dimensions and Newton work. Malformed inputs, exhausted work or a failed physical
solve return the existing structured refusal. A completed fit that fails an acceptance
gate retains candidate parameters, reasons and predictions with **null material and
selection**. It cannot be assigned to a pattern.

The `--allow-estimates` flag controls consumption of the fitted output and overrides
`input.allow_estimates` in the effective recorded request. It does not grant permission
to use estimated acquisition observations: those require the separate input permission
`allow_estimated_observations`. Changing output consent changes the effective request
and frozen selection identities; it does not change fitted coefficients or residuals.
Explicit `replace_existing` consent is required to replace existing equilibrium evidence. Either
nonlinear parameterization may replace a scalar or nonlinear law while retaining its inherited support;
scalar-only calibration cannot replace a nonlinear law, and history laws remain unsupported.

## Replay the accepted result without changing source identities

The CLI returns the complete `calibration`, `selection`, exact `selection_json`, and
independent `simulation_ready` / `mechanics_refusal` fields. Accepted coefficients are
ordinary estimated material properties, so all existing material binding and simulation
consumers use the same source authority. A fit alone does not supply missing bending,
thickness, friction or thermal evidence. The bundled synthetic example therefore remains
incomplete for a garment simulation even when its membrane fit is accepted.

This example runs the synthetic fit and constructs a forward request using the exact
selection serialization returned by Rust:

```sh
ftx-materials calibrate-strip tests/fixtures/materials/synthetic-clamped-strip.json \
  --allow-estimates > /tmp/strip-fit.json
python3 - <<'PY'
import json
from pathlib import Path
report = json.loads(Path('/tmp/strip-fit.json').read_text())
assert report['calibration']['accepted']
data = report['calibration']['input']
request = ('{"schema":"ftx-strip-experiment/1","material":' + report['selection_json'] +
           ',"fixture":' + json.dumps(data['experiments'][0]['fixture']) +
           ',"extensions":[0,0.03],"controls":' + json.dumps(data['controls']) + '}')
Path('/tmp/strip-replay.json').write_text(request)
PY
ftx-materials strip-experiment /tmp/strip-replay.json
```

Reuse `selection_json` directly when assembling material bindings. Reformatting embedded
source numbers in a browser can invalidate a frozen identity. A rejected fit has no such
selection; keep its diagnostics and change only independently justified inputs or policy.

## Use the browser material workbench

In the material workbench, choose **Clamped strip forces and transverse widths** under
material identification, load the acquisition JSON, and explicitly choose whether to admit
the fitted estimates. The worker runs the same `strip::fit` authority locally through
`material_calibrate_strip(input, allow_estimates)`.

The result separates FIT and CHECK errors and displays the selected model's coefficients with their
search bounds, actual strip dimensions and warp axes, midpoint-width usage, local FIT
strain support, stationary starts, forward-work use, mesh-refinement error and enforced-domain
replay. A prediction table retains each acquisition/observation identity alongside actual
force, midpoint width and residuals. Acquisition methods and rejection reasons are plain text.

An accepted result supplies the exact frozen selection to the ordinary pattern-assignment
controls; independent capability refusals stay visible. A completed but unaccepted fit retains
the original input and diagnostic report without an assignable selection. A physical or budget
refusal, changed request, changed response mode or changed estimate policy withdraws stale
results and selections. Report, original input and exact accepted selection are downloadable.

The file-free `material_strip_experiment(input)` export also provides the canonical forward
experiment for browser integrations. Both exports share the native source, model, geometry and
work guards. The frontend checks execute the compiled WASM through the real worker and compare
the complete synthetic calibration and exact frozen selection with native output; lifecycle
tests separately cover source-preserving downloads and rejected replacements. These checks do
not establish empirical textile accuracy or a graphical browser rendering guarantee.

## Matched published knit acquisitions

The existing acquisition parser retains the Sperl 2022 force histories and matched
transverse-contraction CSVs for SJ1, SJ3 and DKIN8, including original bytes, hashes,
metadata and source README. See [measured-knit-strips.md](measured-knit-strips.md).

The reported contraction is `1 - width / reference_width`, but the publication does not
identify the exact width station or statistic. Do not automatically turn those CSV values
into the solver's midpoint chord observations. An explicit, independently justified
measurement/model correspondence is needed. The full source curves also contain large
deformations and unloading; a reversible planar elastic model may be inadequate even when its
directional response is nonlinear.
No built-in measured knit is promoted to a simulation-ready law by this interface.

Independent coupon data, source-specific model adequacy, matched bending/contact/thermal
properties and actual garment validation remain acceptance work under `ftx-ki1` and the
materials/garment gates in the comprehensive plan.

## Timed loading, holds and unloading

`ftx_drape::strip::history::evaluate` accepts a separate `ftx-strip-history/1` request for
experiments where the time path and material memory matter. It uses the same production membrane,
implicit dynamic solver, source-qualified Maxwell spectrum or permanent-strain return map as draping.
The elastic strip and inverse-identification interfaces retain their existing static meaning.

```json
{
  "schema": "ftx-strip-history/1",
  "material": { "id": "your-characterized-sheet", "allow_estimates": false },
  "fixture": {
    "gauge_length_m": 0.1,
    "width_m": 0.06,
    "warp_axis": [0, 1],
    "cells": [2, 2]
  },
  "loading": [
    { "time_s": 0, "extension_fraction": 0 },
    { "time_s": 0.05, "extension_fraction": 0.02 },
    { "time_s": 0.15, "extension_fraction": 0.02 },
    { "time_s": 0.2, "extension_fraction": 0 }
  ],
  "controls": {
    "max_step_s": 0.005,
    "max_steps": 512,
    "velocity_tolerance_m_s": 1e-9,
    "force_tolerance_n": 1e-6,
    "max_newton": 100
  }
}
```

The material ID above is a placeholder; provide an actual frozen selection. The geometry and loading
numbers illustrate a synthetic experiment, not a measured acquisition. The first loading point is
always the stationary, pristine `(0 s, 0 extension)` state. Increasing extensions load, repeated
extensions hold, and decreasing extensions unload. Separation changes linearly between the supplied
times. Every interval is subdivided to the explicit maximum step; the complete path shares one step
budget. At most 1,024 steps, 262,144 retained vertex states and 2,097,152 retained
geometry/material-history values are admitted; the last budget also counts Maxwell branches.

Real source-qualified **areal density** is required because interior sheet inertia is active. Missing
bending, contact, thickness or thermal observations do not prevent this membrane-only experiment.
The material's evidence policy, strain domain and specimen-qualified history remain authoritative.
Simultaneous Maxwell/permanent-strain memory requires an explicitly qualified
[parallel network](material-parallel-memory.md); an unqualified combination still refuses.

The returned `ftx-strip-history-report/1` freezes the complete input and records every accepted time
step, both grip reactions, midpoint width, positions, velocities, local Green-strain bounds, independent
momentum residuals and Newton diagnostics. Stored membrane energy, free-interior kinetic energy,
physical Maxwell dissipation and irreversible plastic work are distinct ledgers. Grip reactions are
membrane forces and exclude fixture/grip inertia. The existing backward-Euler material work is not
silently turned into thermal heating or exact external boundary work. A late domain, numerical or
budget failure returns a refusal without a successful partial report or modified input.

This experiment constrains out-of-plane motion. Negative reactions during unloading describe that
planar constraint; they do not predict free-sheet wrinkles or buckling. Refine time step and mesh
independently. The tests compare low-inertia synthetic coupons with analytical Green-strain bar
reactions, storage and irreversible work, and compare step refinement with the continuous Maxwell
finite-ramp/hold solution. They also check actual density-dependent dynamics and frozen replay.
These are numerical checks, not acquired-textile identification or garment validation.

The timed report's `replay_request_json` contains the exact Rust-serialized frozen request used by
`input_sha256`; browser downloads preserve that string without reserializing its floating-point values.
